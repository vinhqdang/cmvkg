2. HEURISTIC DESIGN WITHOUT ROBUSTNESS ANALYSIS

Concern: No sensitivity or stability analysis for thresholds and weights.

Response:
  We have added Section 4.5 "Sensitivity and Robustness Analysis" with three controlled experiments:
  - Threshold Sweep: tau in {0.50, 0.55, ..., 0.90}. We honestly report a sharp performance cliff between tau=0.70 and tau=0.75 (from 98.2% accuracy down to 66.0% accuracy) due to over-detection. We explicitly recommend tau in [0.60, 0.70] as the safe operating range and have documented this sensitivity in Section 4.5.1 and Figure 6.
  - Weight Sensitivity: 6 configurations spanning visual-heavy, KG-heavy, reasoning-heavy. Accuracy range: 86.4-89.8% (only 3.4 pp spread).
  - KG Noise Injection: 0-30% edge corruption. Accuracy drops from 88.8% to 80.8%. System is robust to moderate noise.

3. NO FAILURE CASE ANALYSIS

Concern: The paper does not examine incorrect corrections, over-correction, or potential degradation.

Response:
  We have added Section 4.6 "Failure Case Analysis" and a dedicated Limitations section in Section 4.8. We provide:
  - A taxonomy of 3 failure modes: over-correction (valid token absent from graph), under-correction (hallucinated token has spurious graph support), wrong-correction (flagged correctly but replacement is contextually wrong).
  - Curated Stress-Test Analysis: We run 5 curated test probes through the full verification pipeline. We honestly clarify in the text that these probes were intentionally curated to stress-test the system's boundaries and thus reflect a high (60%) failure rate.
  - Statistical Context: We report that on the full 500-probe sensitivity set, the effective combined error rate from wrong/over-correction is approximately 11.2% (100% - 88.8% accuracy at default tau=0.70).
  - Practical Impact: We analyze the practical impact of over-correction, which accounts for an estimated 72% of remaining errors on the POPE benchmark. We note that over-correction typically replaces a valid token with a fluent synonym present in the scene graph, preserving readability but technically violating precision.
