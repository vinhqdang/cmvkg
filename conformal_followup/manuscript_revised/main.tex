\title{Certified Correction of Object Hallucinations in Vision--Language Models:\\
Guarantees, Gains and Failure Modes under a Pre-specified Protocol}
\author{Anonymous Author(s)}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Selective prediction gives a vision--language model (VLM) a distribution-free guarantee on its emitted answers
at the price of abstaining on the rest. When the base error rate $\mu$ exceeds the target $\alpha$, any
predictor that only emits or abstains the model's own answer must abstain on at least $(\mu-\alpha)/(1-\alpha)$ of
inputs. For object-existence questions we study a predictor that may emit a different answer: Certified-Correction Risk Control (CCRC) accepts the
model's answer where a correctness score is high, substitutes the answer of a distinct evidence source where that source is decisive, abstains otherwise, and certifies the error rate of
everything emitted with a fixed-sequence Clopper--Pearson test. We prove validity for policy families indexed by
pre-specified score values under i.i.d.\ sampling of calibration units, and evaluate under a protocol in which the
proved and evaluated procedures coincide: folds are unions of images, and thresholds, repair gate and sequence start
are fixed before calibration. On six benchmark--backbone cells, certified coverage over filtering rises by $0.8$ to $7.3$
points with a gate fixed on external data at the target's calibration size, positive in all twelve combinations; an image-level bootstrap excludes zero in seven. Most repaired items re-emit the model's own answer with
detector confirmation. The correctness score supplies most of the coverage; fixed-sequence testing is fragile with small calibration folds. A small matched-prefix experiment suggests that repairing a claim mid-caption increases downstream
hallucination.
\end{abstract}
